We all know that the hackers' sedentary, pizza and coffee filled lives are far from healthy. It's true that this image is very stereotypical but even so, sitting behind your computer for long periods of time is just not what human body was meant to do. At least not yet, I'm sure well somehow evolve :) 

But what can we do about it? I hope to answer a few parts of this large puzzle with this posts. 

<!-- more -->

## The motivation

I have been very healthy during my college years and also the first years of my first job. I did not spend even one day in bed for about 9 or 10 years - no flu could catch me! Although I always carried some excess fat I always had some sort of a excercise routine built into my days. First there was basketball, then I started cycling after getting hooked on it during the student exchange in Denmark, then jogging (even ran a half marathon). But then came a few more stressfull years that included switching jobs, joining a startup, purchasing and completing a house. 

Luckily my wake up call wasn't anything drastical like hearth problems or any other lifestyle disease. I just started getting sick with the rest of the people around me. Flu finally caught up! While I already had some good habits formed I did need to start exercising and paying more attention to my diet. Especially the ammount since the quality was already quite ok, thanks to my girlfriend.

So here is my view on what is important to to avoid most of the problems that a hacker can face.

## Working environment

### Chair and table

I agree with Jeff Atwood about the [importance of a quality desk and chair](http://blog.codinghorror.com/computer-workstation-ergonomics):
>  A quality desk and quality chair will be some of the best investments you'll ever make as a software developer. 

If you are responsible for your buying your own equipment go out there and try out a couple of chairs and tables and see which ones suits you best. Now, this does not have to be one of the expensive Aeon chairs or motorized height adjusting tables. 

Just make sure that you can adjust it to your own needs and with acordance to [workstation ergonomics guidelines](http://blog.codinghorror.com/computer-workstation-ergonomics/). A good chair/table will last quite a few years so it's cost will spread over the years that you will use it and might be as low a $10 per month or even lower.

If your employer suplies you with one make sure that it is good enough. I think you should insist on getting a better chair/table if the current one causes you any trouble.
